# SILVEROAK CIRCULAR ECONOMY & WASTE-TO-ENERGY — V1 CONCEPT
Parent: MASTER DEVLOPMAN → Construction & Manufacturing / Agriculture & Energy / Transportation / RevenueOS.
Date: 2026-10-17. Status: planning; no collection contracts, trucks, permits, fuel output or investments confirmed.

## Project outcomes
1. Municipal waste collection and transfer network in Haiti and Dominican Republic.
2. Materials recovery: aluminum, steel, glass, paper and appropriate plastics.
3. Segregated organic feedstock to industrial anaerobic digestion; biogas first for electricity/heat, optionally upgraded to certified renewable natural gas (RNG) suitable for permitted CNG vehicles.
4. Used cooking oil through licensed processors into specification-tested biodiesel; not directly suitable for all diesel engines.
5. Only after environmental and fuel testing, evaluate industrial plastic pyrolysis as an independent feasibility study, not as guaranteed road diesel.
6. Never combine hazardous or biomedical waste into ordinary municipal stream.

## Fleet design (requirements, not purchasing list)
- Local waste pickup: enclosed refuse compactor trucks (service zones and road constraints).
- Long-haul: road-legal tractor units + covered, leakproof transfer trailers and container roll-offs; do not haul uncovered municipal waste.
- Sorted recyclables: balers and box trailers; containerized scrap haulage.
- Food waste: washable sealed trucks or leakproof bins.
- Bulky waste: hook-lift/roll-off trucks; loader/skid-steer or forklifts in transfer yards.
- Fuel and digestate transportation: regulated licensed tankers ONLY once permits, appropriate certifications and route controls exist.
- Trucks may later use certified RNG/CNG with suitable vehicles and filling infrastructure; RNG is not a direct liquid diesel substitute.
- Capacity, quantity and wheelbase must be driven by weighed daily throughput, haul distance, duty cycles, road loading limits, fuel economics and fleet maintenance capacity. Do not commit to buying dozens before proof of feedstock.

## Process flow
household/commercial source sorting → permitted local pickup → transfer scales and waste audit → contamination screening → material recovery →
(a) recyclable contracts;
(b) clean organics → engineered anaerobic digesters → gas cleaning/CHP → possible RNG upgrading, compression and vehicle filling;
(c) approved used cooking oil → certified biodiesel processor;
(d) non-recoverable residues → lawful disposal.
Risk: methane leaks, H2S, fires, oxygen-deficient spaces, leachate, hazardous contaminants, traffic accidents; plan safety by licensed engineers and environmental professionals.

## Pilot phases
P0 0–90 days: waste composition/tonnage studies; route survey; municipal permissions; feedstock MoUs; weighbridge and end-market quotes; environmental screening; community consultation.
P1 pilot 1: one collection district, leased trucks where viable, controlled separation of dry recyclables and organics, auditable mass balance.
P2 pilot 2: bench/industrial feasibility and feedstock lab analysis for anaerobic digestion + power/heat; preliminary gas demand and offtake.
P3 conditional: design/build permitted AD site; supervised operation and monitoring; RNG and biodiesel only if viable certified offtake.
P4 conditional: expand provinces and cross-country networks with separate national legal entities/licensing.

## Economics
One-way ownership model initially: leasing and service partners before capital-intensive truck purchase. Budget categories: site and land, permits, EIA, legal, bin fleet, collection, weighbridge, sorting, staff, insurance, maintenance, fuel, tires, workshop, odor/leachate controls, biogas equipment, gas cleanup, environmental monitoring and contingency.
Revenues must be separately verified: tipping fees, municipal service contracts, recyclable sales, energy offtake, fertilizer meeting standards, fuel sales only if licensed. Model sensitivity to tonnage, contamination, uptime, off-take, diesel costs and exchange rate.

## Compliance
Dominican Republic: Ley 225-20 and implementing regulations govern solid waste collection, transport, recovery and facilities. Verify Ministry of Environment permits, municipal agreements, transport/weight regulations, air permits, fuel standards and inspections.
Haiti: identify current applicable environment, municipality, transport, energy/fuel authorization and actual operating conditions with local counsel and competent authorities; do not assume permits cross borders.
Any AD and fuel system requires independent engineering design, occupational safety and emergency procedures; NO DIY pressure-vessel or untested fuel blending.

## Supplier RFQ minimum
Country, permitted service area, actual tonnes/day by waste fraction, collection routes, road type/load rules, equipment throughput, warranty, spare parts and local maintenance, training, safety compliance, emissions testing, fuel specifications, installation and operations costs, financing/lease terms and reference installations.
