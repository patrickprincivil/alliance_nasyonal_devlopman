# SILVEROAK DIGITAL SOLUTIONS — MASTER DEVLOPMAN
## Welding Simulation & Industrial Training Center V1.0
**Source plan supplied:** 21 October 2026. **Status:** planning only; no equipment ordered, vendor partnership, accredited courses or certificates.

### Placement within unified architecture
- Education / Silveroak Business Academy → Welding & Industrial Skills Academy
- Manufacturing / Metal Fabrication & Robotics → practical fabrication workshop
- AI Operations → learning analytics, equipment scheduling and human-reviewed reporting
- Construction / Haiti Smart Vertical City → future workforce and fabrication applications
Use shared identity, projects, budgeting, procurement, document storage, training platform, approvals and audit. Do not create a duplicate standalone platform.

### Vendor evaluation
Seabery Welding Simulator (previous Soldamatic) is a proprietary AR-supported training product. Treat manufacturer's reported improvements in training efficiency, cost, accident reduction and carbon footprint as **vendor claims**, not independent results or a guarantee.

Evaluate support for MIG/MAG, TIG and SMAW; available physical training implements, exercises, instructor workstation, offline operation, data exports and accessibility. Request an authorized demonstration and a formal quotation. Do not reproduce or reverse-engineer proprietary simulator software.

### Pilot design
1. Obtain 2–3 training-needs interviews from employers/instructors in target region.
2. Request vendor quotation: simulator configuration, purchase vs subscription, software license, instructor onboarding, spares, warranty, service-level support, import charges, shipping, taxes, power and offline features.
3. Check local training regulations and employer-recognized certification pathways; simulator alone does not issue accredited welder certification.
4. Set up a supervised 1-simulator lab with instructor workstation, safe power, secure storage and appropriate network.
5. Run a small training cohort, starting with simulator practice; progress to separate conventional welding lab **only** after appropriate facility design, ventilation, electrical/fire controls, PPE, instructors and regulatory review.
6. Capture consented attendance, practice hours, skill rubric, simulator-to-physical transfer, safety incidents, equipment uptime and direct cost per learner.
7. Require human review of readiness for practical welding; never let an AI performance score substitute for welding qualification.

### Implementation gates
- P0: assign program owner and document safety, procurement and consent.
- P1: gather vendor quote, identify local accredited training partner.
- P2: evaluate facility readiness and fund one simulator.
- P3: conduct supervised pilot and record measurable outcomes.
- P4: independent evaluation and scale/no-scale decision.

### Security and compliance
Protect trainee records, verify any data transfer to vendor cloud, obtain consent, set deletion/retention periods, restrict instructor/admin roles. Automated marketing, payments, procurement, certification claims and facility activation require human approval.

### Cross-project dependencies
- Business Academy: curriculum, teacher training and learner progression.
- Metal Fabrication: material handling and inspected weld practice.
- Robotics Precast: future maintenance and production technician skills.
- Smart Vertical City: future infrastructure skills, subject to engineering standards.
- AI Operations: non-sensitive training analytics and audit.

### Acceptance tests
- Signed vendor quotation and licensing verified.
- Written local safety and regulatory review.
- Instructor confirms simulator exercise quality, uptime and offline behavior.
- At least one pilot cohort measured against defined starting competencies.
- No unsupported claims of official certification or actual savings.
- All equipment and purchase decisions approved in Master Control Center.
