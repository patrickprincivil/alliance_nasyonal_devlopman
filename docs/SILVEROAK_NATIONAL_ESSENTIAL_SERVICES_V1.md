# SILVEROAK MASTER DEVLOPMAN — ESSENTIAL SERVICES & SOCIAL INFRASTRUCTURE V1
Date: 17 October 2026. Countries: Haiti and Dominican Republic. Status: **planning only**, not funded, licensed, or deployed.

## Vision
Integrated national-development program to expand access to reliable electricity, safe water, affordable nutritious food, livelihood opportunities and dignified housing. Universal national coverage is a long-term objective, not a claim of service availability.

## Do not duplicate existing Master modules
Reuse existing Agriculture/Energy, Construction/Infrastructure, Foundation/Charity, Business/Revenue, Telecom/Wi-Fi, Health, Education and Master Control Center services. Cross-sector coordination is a program, not a standalone platform.

## Five coordinated service lines
1. **Power for communities and microbusiness:** solar PV + battery + appropriately engineered inverter/protection, backed by grid/generator only when justified. Separate tariffs for households and commercial cold chains; subsidize vulnerable households via donors or explicit cross-subsidy. A solar system requires energy demand/peak load analysis, batteries and safe wiring; cannot promise 24/7 without engineering.
2. **Safe water and sanitation:** survey source yield and seasonal variation, test microbial and chemical safety, select treatment (filtration/disinfection; desalination only where needed), water storage, metering, distribution and maintenance. Publish periodic test results, plan sewage/wastewater and drainage. No untreated water marketed as potable.
3. **Community food systems:** farm procurement, storage, refrigerated cold chain, community kitchens, professional low-cost restaurants, fritay vendors and affordable buffet operations. Nutrition, safe food handling, employee pay and realistic portion-cost model. Subsidized or free meals for eligible beneficiaries, funded transparently; never assume commercial buffet can be unlimited without portion/waste controls.
4. **Dignified housing:** low-cost incremental homes and safe rental/shelter paths linked to potable water, sanitation, power, accessibility, fire safety, land title/leases and local building permits. Licensed engineers/contractors must validate designs and structural safety.
5. **Employment and local enterprise:** paid training, small-vendor stalls, kitchen incubation, equipment leasing where viable, maintenance apprenticeships and farmer purchasing contracts. Avoid promising jobs or finance before secured budgets.

## Economic model: separate sustainability and charity
- Commercial electricity, water and food customers may pay transparent tariffs to cover service costs; free or discounted essential services need grants, donors or verified subsidies.
- Food programs: (ingredients + fuel/electricity + labor + transport + hygiene + packaging + food waste + depreciation) / meals served = true unit cost. Pricing below true cost requires a documented funding source.
- Maintain distinct restricted-donation vs commercial ledgers and lawful tax/charity status. Avoid stating tax deductible status until authorized.
- No compulsory service purchases; disclose fixed fees, deposits, connection fees and contract terms.

## Pilot designs, not promises
Pilot DR: San Pedro de Macorís / Los Llanos, subject to site consent, permitting, suppliers, grid/water surveys and safety checks.
Pilot Haiti: Jacmel / Morne-à-Brûlé, subject to land rights, local security, access, hydrology, medical and construction approvals.
Each pilot targets one shared community service site with solar, filtered potable water, affordable kitchen, food storage, Wi-Fi and a small housing demonstration only after land/permitting review.

## Gates before procurement
1. Community consultation, needs assessment and beneficiary protection.
2. Land/title/right-to-use review and country-specific permits.
3. Energy load profile, solar resource, wiring and electrical design by qualified professional.
4. Groundwater/municipal source survey, lab water testing, treatment and waste management.
5. Kitchen inspection and food safety certification; sanitation, allergens and temperature logs.
6. Housing permits, geotechnical/structural review and accessibility.
7. Total cost of ownership: CAPEX, OPEX, repairs, spare parts, insurance, customs, financing and subsidy.
8. Audited nonprofit/commercial accounting and consent/privacy protection.
9. Procurement comparison and independent engineering review.
10. Publish measured uptime, water test results, meals served, affordability and beneficiary outcomes with no sensitive individual data.

## Shared data and controls
Use one organization-scoped PostgreSQL data model after existing schema audit: sites, feasibility_studies, energy_assets, meter_readings, water_tests, water_assets, kitchens, meal_programs, meal_inventory, housing_units, residents (restricted access), beneficiaries (restricted), subsidies, grants, vendors, maintenance_tickets, approvals, audit_events.
Keep medical, child and beneficiary information separate with strict roles and retention controls. Payments and subsidy decisions require human review and fraud checks.

## Rollout
Phase 0: local research, partner introductions, technical design and budget.
Phase 1: one micro-pilot in each country only if finance, permission and safety gates pass.
Phase 2: replicate independently verified units to districts, track affordability and maintenance.
Phase 3: regional interconnection and wholesale procurement after proof of service.
Phase 4: nationwide ambitions only with regulator, operators and sustained multi-year funding.

## Core dashboard indicators
Energy uptime, cost/kWh, safety incidents; water liters/day, lab-test compliance, household access; meal count, nutrient adequacy, cost/meal, waste; housing occupancy, inspection compliance, maintenance; local jobs, subsidy coverage and cash runway.

Status: plan and schema proposal only. No installed power plant, water system, kitchen, social housing or national network is claimed.
